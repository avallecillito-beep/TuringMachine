Selected Machine

Binary Increment

In Turing machines, binary increment is an operation that adds 1 to a binary number on the tape by scanning to the rightmost bit and moving left to propagate the carry—flipping 1s to 0s until it turns a 0 (or a leading blank space) into a 1 and halts.

Example: 1011 → 1100

Unary Multiplication

In Turing machines, integer multiplication is an arithmetic operation that computes the product of two integers encoded on the tape—most commonly in unary or binary notation—by systematically repeating addition. Depending on the encoding, the machine typically achieves this by copying the multiplicand onto a separate section of the tape once for each unit of the multiplier (for unary), or by using bit-shifting and conditional binary addition (for binary) until the multiplier is reduced to zero and only the final product remains.

3 x 4 | Input: 11101111

Unary Addition

In Turing machines, unary addition is an operation that adds two numbers represented as strings of 1s separated by a delimiter by replacing the separator with a 1 and erasing the final 1 at the end of the combined string.

111011 → 11111
